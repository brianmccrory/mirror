The name Gekkasha is written in Japanese as 月花舎, three kanji letters that represent moon, flower, and hut or inn. This is the striking sign you’ll see as you approach Gekkasha’s entrance from the busy street outside.

t38:

-   (COFFEE 1) 2/18/2025 afternoon coffee
    -   Gekkasha in Jimbocho/Kudansha to check it out, coffee (not a live show). Interesting, peaceful space. Good audio sound, from stereo and speakers at inside. Not a cramped feeling at all.
-   (LIVE 1) 2/19/2025	Hashizume Ryosuke	duo with piano Ogaeri Ami
    -   jazz bars vs clubs vs kissa vs spaces vs spots vs live houses vs jazz/performance art venues, etc…
    -   The 1st set (45 mins) was free improv which eventually turned into “Both Sides Now”. 2nd set seemed to be completely improvised.
    -   former place (Chakaiki? 茶会記)
-   (LIVE 2) 2/24/2025	Nishijima Toru	trio with saxophone Kanon Aonami and piano Fumi Endo
    -   For something different, this slow, spacey, improvised music is fine, but I don’t see it becoming a habit (nor was I compelled to buy their CDs, which seemed more expensive than average).
    -   It can put people to sleep, or at least in a different (altered?) state of mind. But, mild, non-noisy, and experimental, for people who are exploring and maybe tired of the same old things.
    -   Gekkasha - same owner, different (previous) location as Kissa Chakaiki (Yotsuya Sanchome)　喫茶茶会記.
    -   Ryosuke Hashizume would perform as “Tactile Sounds” there.
-   (COFFEE 2) 3/11/2025
    -   Next, walked to Gekkasha for a second cup of coffee. here, beans are freshly ground as well. Although I could listen to jazz BGM in the front, there was also a studio rental in the lower level (basement?) where some musicians were rehearsing for an upcoming performance (flamenco guitar?)... which is a distraction for people coming here expecting to listen to jazz a la “jazz kissa” style. (It is more like a study room or library-like annex.) ... listening to two music sources at the same time. To be fair, the printed schedule of events that is passed out does say 13:30~17:00 is the time for “book cafe/studio rental.”
    -   It’s also a bit dim inside, which is a nice mood for dozing, but hard to read/write unless you have excellent eyesight. (I found a brighter place to sit under a light. The master raised the light level later, as another customer came in.)
    -   When the rehearsal ended, and jazz BGM played, it is a relaxing and comfortable place. Spacious, peaceful, low-key and low stress (high ceilings and spaciousness) and the low-key master who wears a beret but doesn’t seem to make much eye contact; shy, quiet. Also, table space allows for spreading out of papers/books like on a desk, more space than is available at typical Tokyo coffee shops where space is tight.
    -   Conversation is also ok (master and a customer were having a conversation while I worked on my schedule) to while away the hours before you realize it.
    -   700 yen for coffee at both places (Bigboy, Gekkasha).
